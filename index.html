Concrete is more than cement and water. I'm exploring the materials, proportions, and testing decisions behind a successful mix—and asking people in the field what matters most.

01 / Starting with the basics

What goes into concrete?

Most conventional concrete starts with the same fundamental components. The quantities, quality, and interaction of these ingredients shape how the material behaves when fresh and after it hardens.

Educational overview only. Mix proportions should be developed and verified for the intended application using appropriate standards and testing.

02 / The balancing act

A good mix is designed for a purpose.

There is no single perfect mix. A sidewalk, bridge element, and concrete canoe can require very different priorities. Good mix design balances competing goals and considers real placement conditions.

Start with the application

What strength, workability, exposure resistance, density, and construction conditions does the project actually need?

Key decisions to investigate
  • Water-to-cementitious-materials ratioImportant to strength and permeability, but must be balanced with workability.
  • Aggregate grading and proportioningAffects packing, paste demand, handling, and consistency.
  • Admixture selectionCan change flow, air content, setting time, and other properties.
  • Placement and curingEven a promising mix may perform poorly if it is placed or cured improperly.

Learning by asking better questions.

I'm Dani Bayyat, a civil engineering student at San José State University and a member of ASCE's Concrete Canoe mix team. This site is a place to document what I'm learning about how concrete is designed and tested—and what practicing professionals can teach me.

01 / Starting with the basics

What goes into concrete?

A lot of concrete mixes start with the same basic ingredients. The interesting part is how changing the amounts and materials can change the final result.

I'm sharing what I learn here, not a ready-to-use mix recipe. Real projects need proper mix design, standards, and testing.

02 / The balancing act

So what actually makes a mix good?

That's the big question I'm trying to answer. A mix that's great for a sidewalk might be totally wrong for a concrete canoe. It depends on what you're building and what matters most.

First, what are we making?

Do we care most about strength, weight, ease of pouring, durability, or some combination? The goal changes the mix.

Things I'm looking into
  • Water-to-cementitious-materials ratioThis can affect strength and how easily water moves through concrete, but adding less water can make a mix harder to place.
  • Aggregate grading and proportioningThe sizes and amounts of sand and rock can change how tightly particles pack and how the fresh mix handles.
  • Admixture selectionThese can make concrete flow differently, set faster or slower, or behave better in certain conditions.
  • Placement and curingA well-designed mix still needs good placement and curing to perform the way it's supposed to.
